The Backstop That Became a Bypass

The short, simple version of the full case file.

Posted Oct. 28, 2026 · Updated Oct. 28, 2026
Want every detail and source? Read the full case file

The short version

  • On 1/1/2026, GRR-Lost Creek, LLC and E B R Management, LLC (both formed 8/25/2025) filed a preliminary municipality petition for Lost Creek: 490.53 acres of Garff Rogers Ranch land in Browns Canyon, Summit County, with Ivory Development running the project.
  • On 2/5/2026 the state declined the petition because Utah's 2-per-year statewide cap was already reached. Ivory then asked Summit County for a standard rezone of up to 3,000 units across 407–600 acres, which got a negative planning commission recommendation on 8/7/2026 after 31 residents spoke against it.
  • H.B. 510 (2026) would have given counties a coordination period, a traffic study right, and an audit-and-dispute right, but never a vote. It passed the House 68–0–7 and failed in the Senate 14–12–3, one Yea short of the 15 needed.
  • Four senators with Ivory Homes contributions on file voted no and a fifth, Sen. Lincoln Fillmore, did not vote. The bill's Senate sponsor, Sen. Kirk Cullimore, also an Ivory Homes donee, voted yes. The record does not show why any senator voted as they did.
  • A year earlier, H.B. 37 (2025) changed the town population floor in §10-2a-201.5 from 100 to 75, but left a separate bar on areas with "fewer than 100 people" untouched. Sen. Fillmore carried that bill in the Senate, and Steve Waldrip testified for it.

What it is

A 2024 Utah law, S.B. 258, lets one, two, or three landowners ask the state to make their land a new town. This is called a "preliminary municipality." The county does not get a vote. The Lieutenant Governor's office decides.

Lost Creek is land in Browns Canyon, Summit County, near Park City and Hideout. It belongs to a family ranch, Garff Rogers Ranch. Two companies, GRR-Lost Creek, LLC and E B R Management, LLC, were formed on 8/25/2025. They filed to make Lost Creek a town on 1/1/2026. Ivory Development, led by President Chris Gamvroulas, runs the project. He called the filing a "backstop."

What the records show

  • The petition covered 490.53 acres, 565 homes, townhomes and cottages, and 642 residents at full buildout.
  • Utah takes only 2 of these petitions a year, statewide. The cap was already full. On 2/5/2026 the state declined Lost Creek. It was timing only.
  • Ivory then asked Summit County for a normal rezone: up to 3,000 units on 407–600 acres. On 8/7/2026 the planning commission recommended against it. 31 residents spoke against it. This is the normal county process, with public notice and a public hearing.
  • H.B. 510 (2026) would have made developers work with the county first. It added a traffic study right and a right to audit and dispute the numbers. It did not give the county a vote. It passed the House 68–0. It failed in the Senate 14–12. It needed 15.

The rule that changed a year earlier

H.B. 37 (2025) was a housing bill. Its primary sponsor was Rep. James A. Dunnigan. Sen. Lincoln Fillmore carried it in the Senate. One part changed the smallest town size from 100 people to 75.

But a few lines down, the same law still says an area with fewer than 100 people cannot become a town. That line was not changed. So the real floor did not move.

Steve Waldrip, the governor's housing advisor, spoke for H.B. 37 at the House (Feb. 13, 2025) and Senate (Feb. 27, 2025) hearings.

Why it matters

  • Of four 2026 town requests, three were turned down by a yearly quota or a map line, not by a county. Had Lost Creek been filed a few weeks earlier, nothing in the law would have stopped it.
  • The same small group that can form a town can also form a tax district (a PID). A PID needs 100% of landowners to sign. On unsold land, that is the same one to three owners. No PID is on record for Lost Creek. The law makes it possible. It has not been shown here.
  • A PID usually pays only for things inside the development. Roads and services outside it can fall on people who already live there, unless every impact fee is charged and collected.

What we don't know yet

  • When the Summit County Council will vote on the Lost Creek rezone.
  • Whether Lost Creek will be filed again as a town in a later year.
  • Whether any PID will ever be filed on this land.
  • Whether the other senators took Ivory Homes money. They have not been checked yet.

Questions to ask

  • Summit County Council: When will you vote on the Lost Creek rezone, and will the public get to speak?
  • Lieutenant Governor's Office: How is filing order set for the 2-a-year cap, and can Lost Creek file again?
  • Your state senator (le.utah.gov): Will a county-oversight bill come back, and will it give counties a real vote?
  • Your state senator: After H.B. 37, is the town floor 75 people or 100?
  • Summit County: Has any PID petition been filed for this land, and will every impact fee be collected?
