The Trustee Signed His Own Deal

The short, simple version of the full case file.

Posted Oct. 28, 2026 · Updated Oct. 28, 2026
Want every detail and source? Read the full case file

The short version

  • Nordic Valley Land Associates LLC (NVLA), run by two entities that both list Gage Froerer as manager, transferred its land to Nordic Village Venture LLC for zero consideration on Jan. 27, 2023. Froerer filed J&G's dissolution 11 days later; NVLA and Kier Development were dissolved Jan. 20, 2026, after the PID bonds closed.
  • Froerer filed no written conflict disclosure for NVLA from 2018 until Nov. 20, 2022, after a citizen complaint to the State Auditor. A handwritten note on his February 2023 form says he divested; property and corporate records do not support that. He filed no disclosure before the Nov. 20, 2024 PID creation vote.
  • PID trustee Brandon Henrie, president of Clyde's GWC Capital division, signed a parcel from GWC Capital into the PID he oversees for $10. Trustee Laurent Jouffray's own disclosure lists a 50% stake through Skyline Mountain Base, with no recusal on record. Trustee Brook Cole's petition claimed 100% ownership when a 45/5/50 split was already on paper.
  • Per Mediapart, French regional president Renaud Muselier, co-president of Solideo Alpes 2030, has been the real owner of Nordic Valley since 2014 but declared himself to France's transparency authority (HATVP) only as an unpaid "manager."
  • Roughly a third of the $120,870,032 project lands on the public: $24,355,615 in PID debt plus $11,525,738 and $5,045,908 in diverted tax increment. The Weber Fire District's own model holds its revenue from this land to $428,158/year during 2027–2041, versus $856,315/year after.

What it is

Nordic Village is a resort and housing project at Nordic Valley in Ogden Valley, Weber County. It sits on about 512 acres.

Weber County created a public infrastructure district, or PID, for it. A PID can borrow money and charge a special levy to the people who later own property inside it. The county approved $80 million in bonding for this one.

This case follows three people and three roles that all touch the same land: a county commissioner, the PID's own trustees, and the resort's owner in France. The goal is not to argue against the resort. It is to check whether the public process had the disclosures and recusals it was supposed to have.

What the records show

  • The commissioner. Gage Froerer is listed as manager of the two entities that ran Nordic Valley Land Associates LLC (NVLA). On Jan. 27, 2023, NVLA's land moved to Nordic Village Venture LLC for zero dollars. Eleven days later, Froerer filed to dissolve one of those entities.
  • Late disclosure. Froerer was elected in 2018. He filed no written conflict form for NVLA until Nov. 20, 2022, after a resident complained to the State Auditor. A handwritten note on his February 2023 form says he divested. Property and corporate records do not support that. He filed no disclosure before the Nov. 20, 2024 vote that created the PID.
  • The $10 deed. Brandon Henrie is a PID trustee and president of Clyde's GWC Capital division. He signed a parcel from GWC Capital into the PID he oversees, for $10.
  • The 50% stake. Trustee Laurent Jouffray's own disclosure says his company, Skyline Mountain Base, owns 50% of the developer. No recusal is on record. County records show he is registered to vote in Box Elder County. State law requires PID trustees to live inside the district.
  • The 100% claim. Trustee Brook Cole signed the PID petition as the 100% owner. A document dated months earlier already showed the real split: 50% Skyline Mountain Base, 45% Clyde Capital Group, 5% John Lewis.
  • The owner in France. The French news site Mediapart reported that Renaud Muselier, a sitting regional president who helps lead France's 2030 Winter Olympics work, has owned Nordic Valley since 2014. He told France's transparency office only that he was an unpaid "manager."

Who pays

The whole project costs $120,870,032. About a third of that lands on the public.

SourceAmountWho pays
Developer money$79,942,771The developer
PID debt$24,355,615Future owners inside the PID, through a special levy
Tax increment (school, water, fire)$11,525,738Growth those districts give up for 15 years
Tax increment (Weber County)$5,045,908Growth the county gives up for 15 years

The Weber Fire District's own table shows it gets $428,158 a year from this land during 2027–2041. After that, it gets $856,315 a year. The gap is the money set aside for the project.

Why it matters

In each finding, the person deciding and a person benefiting were the same, with no recusal on record.

To be fair, some of this was done in the open. Froerer recused from one vote in August 2022. Jouffray's 50% stake was on paper. Henrie's $10 deed is a recorded public deed. Tax increment financing is a standard, legal state tool. And Mediapart's reporting brought the ownership question to the public in France.

The cost still reaches people who will never live in Nordic Village. School, fire and water districts give up growth they would have collected. They must raise rates on everyone else or cut services.

The water is tight, too. Pineview Reservoir, in the middle of the valley, serves roughly 120,000–135,000 people downstream. In 2026, Weber Basin cut allocations by 20% due to drought.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether Froerer holds any other paperwork or partnership interest with Skyline Mountain Base, Sorep or John Lewis beyond what is documented here.
  • Whether Cole, Henrie and David Smith live inside the district, as state law requires of trustees.
  • What the Liberty Village-to-GWC Capital deed listed as its price.
  • Whether Muselier's ownership ever crossed paths with his France-Utah Olympic work beyond his January 2024 trip.

Since this page posted: On Oct. 13, 2026, Weber County's GRAMA appeals board ordered the county to release Froerer's 2025 conflict form without the employer fields blacked out. It let the 2026 redaction stand because he checked the "at-risk" box that year. Laura Warburton, named on the full page, resigned from the Ogden Valley Planning Commission on Jan. 26, 2026.

Questions to ask

  • Weber County Commission: Why did no trustee recuse on the $10 transfer or the bonding votes?
  • Weber County Recorder: What price is listed on the Liberty Village-to-GWC Capital deed?
  • Weber County Clerk/Auditor, Elections: Do the PID trustees live inside the district?
  • Weber Fire District: How will you cover the gap between $428,158 and $856,315 a year?
  • Utah State Auditor: What came of the Oct. 24, 2022 complaint about Froerer's disclosure?
