IN PLAIN WORDS · FOLLOW THE DEED · CASE 04 · FD 600.01

The Applicant Holds Those Rights

The short, simple version of the full case file.

Posted Oct. 28, 2026 · Updated Oct. 28, 2026
Want every detail and source? Read the full case file

The short version

  • In 2023, Keller Cattle Corporation, a 1959 Utah corporation now controlled by House Speaker Mike Schultz, applied for 556.70 acre-feet of new water rights (two applications of 278.35 acre-feet each, 13-4099 and 13-4100) on the Promontory Mountains, seven miles from the Great Salt Lake shore.
  • The Utah Rivers Council filed a timely protest and Farmland Reserve Inc. filed a late letter. The State Engineer approved both applications on July 18, 2024, writing that "even though approval may impair existing water rights, the applicant holds those water rights."
  • A hearing was held Dec. 14, 2023, in Brigham City, with a hearing report, notes and an audio recording on file. The signed July 18, 2024 Order states that a hearing was not held.
  • On Aug. 21, 2023, four days before the applications were filed, Schultz voted as a Utah Inland Port Authority board member to create the Golden Spike project area in Box Elder County, with a $219 million incentive package. He did not recuse, and his 2023 UIPA disclosure lists board positions as "None."
  • Schultz was the primary sponsor of H.B. 491, the state's Great Salt Lake water-policy bill. The process followed statutory notice and protest steps; the open questions are about what the signed Order says and does not address.

What it is

Mike Schultz is the Speaker of the Utah House. He controls a company called Keller Cattle Corporation. It is an old Utah company, first registered in 1959. Schultz did not start it. He bought it.

In 2023, the company asked the state for new water. It filed two applications, 13-4099 and 13-4100. Each one asked for 278.35 acre-feet. Together that is 556.70 acre-feet a year.

The land is on the Promontory Mountains in Box Elder County. It is about seven miles from the shore of the Great Salt Lake.

Schultz was also the primary sponsor of H.B. 491, the state's own Great Salt Lake water bill.

What the records show

  • Filed: Aug. 25, 2023. Approved: July 18, 2024, by State Engineer Teresa Wilhelmsen.
  • Protests: The Utah Rivers Council filed on time. Farmland Reserve Inc., the LDS Church's farmland arm, sent a late letter.
  • The reason given: The signed Order says that "even though approval may impair existing water rights, the applicant holds those water rights."
  • The hearing: A hearing was held on Dec. 14, 2023, in Brigham City. There is a hearing report, two pages of notes and an audio recording. But the signed Order says a hearing was not held.
  • A big well: One well is 16 inches wide and 540 feet deep. It is marked "Existing." Every other well is 8 or 10 inches. No well log is on file for it.
  • Farmland Reserve: It holds older water rights in the same area, going back to 1940. Its letter warned some water might be counted twice. The Order does not answer that warning.

More from the record

  • Golden Spike vote: On Aug. 21, 2023, four days before filing for the water, Schultz voted on the Utah Inland Port Authority board to create the Golden Spike project area in Box Elder County. It came with a $219 million incentive package. He did not recuse.
  • Disclosure forms: In 2019, Schultz listed two board seats. His 2023 UIPA form and his 2026 form say "None," even though he served on the UIPA board in 2023.
  • Business: His House bio says he stepped away from his home building company in 2023. His 2026 form still lists Castle Creek Homes LLC and Mike Schultz Construction Inc as owned and as income sources.
  • The PAC: House Speaker Leadership PAC's treasurer is Kyle Palmer, who owns Irish Elk LLC. The page reported roughly $200,000 to Palmer and Irish Elk. A later check of the same filings found the correct total through the 2026 Convention report is $175,151.63.

Why it matters

Water near the Great Salt Lake is scarce, and the lake is in decline. When a state official approves new water, the public should be able to trust the paperwork.

Here, the signed Order has a fact wrong about the hearing. It also does not answer the warning from the neighbor with older rights. And the applicant is one of the most powerful people in state government.

To be fair: the state did follow its notice and protest steps. People could object, and they did. The questions are about what the final Order says.

What we don't know yet

  • Why the Order says no hearing was held. It could be a typing mistake. That has not been shown either way.
  • Who drilled the 16-inch well, and when.
  • Whether the Chimney Rock hunting unit, where Sawmill Ranch guides elk hunts, sits on this same Promontory land. That has not been checked against the state's map.
  • Whether any Schultz land sits inside or next to the Golden Spike or Stratos project areas.

Questions to ask

  • Utah Division of Water Rights: Why does the Order say no hearing was held? Is there a well log for the 16-inch well? Was Farmland Reserve's warning reviewed?
  • Box Elder County Recorder, UIPA and MIDA: Does any Schultz land sit inside or next to Golden Spike or Stratos?
  • Utah House Chief Clerk: Must public board seats be listed on disclosure forms?
  • Utah Division of Wildlife Resources: Does the Chimney Rock unit include these wells and springs?
