The Chain Behind Wasatch Highlands

The short, simple version of the full case file.

Posted Oct. 28, 2026 · Updated Oct. 28, 2026
Want every detail and source? Read the full case file

The short version

  • SITLA, the state trust lands agency, signed a Mutual Option Agreement (DEVL 1297) on Feb. 28, 2023 with "Mountain Venture Studios" for 720.96 acres in Wasatch County. It is not a sale: SITLA keeps title and agrees to a 10-year development lease only if the county rezones for at least 100 lots and the developer secures water rights for at least 100 lots.
  • "Mountain Venture Studios" is not separately registered in Utah. Records trace it through Philo Development Group, LLC (Utah) to Philo Venture Management, LLC and Philo Venture Partners, LLC (both Delaware). The Utah filing for the last layer left managers/members blank, which is legal; OpenCorporates lists Chad Staheli as manager.
  • SITLA's own board packet appraises the land at $3.6M under current zoning (5 homes max) and projects about $16M if the county grants a rezone to 100+ lots. SITLA's board extended the deadline from Jan. 1, 2025 to Jan. 1, 2027 on Sept. 19, 2024.
  • The plan grew in public records: 100 lots on the state parcel in 2023, a roughly 4,000-acre master plan by Sept. 2024, then a 700+ home preliminary-municipality bid on the private land. Wasatch County formally rejected the 144-lot legislative development agreement on Aug. 19, 2026.
  • Mike Mower, Senior Advisor for Community Outreach and Intergovernmental Relations in Gov. Cox's office, holds SITLA's seventh, at-large board seat "at the pleasure of the Governor," with a term that "does not expire." The board's Aug. 20, 2026 agenda had no item on this parcel.

What it is

Utah owns land to raise money for public schools. A state agency called SITLA manages it. Its board is appointed by the Governor.

In Wasatch County, SITLA owns 720.96 acres. On Feb. 28, 2023, SITLA signed a deal called an option agreement (DEVL 1297) with a company named "Mountain Venture Studios."

It is not a sale. SITLA keeps the land. It agrees to a 10-year development lease only if two things happen first:

  • The county rezones the land so at least 100 lots can be built.
  • The developer gets water rights for at least 100 lots.

What the records show

Who is behind the name. "Mountain Venture Studios" is not registered on its own in Utah. State records lead through three companies:

  • Philo Development Group, LLC (Utah), formed Nov. 14, 2022
  • Philo Venture Management, LLC (Delaware), formed Aug. 8, 2022
  • Philo Venture Partners, LLC (Delaware)

The Utah filing for the last company left the owners blank. That is legal. Delaware does not make owners public. But OpenCorporates lists Chad Staheli, Philo's co-founder and CEO, as its manager.

What the land is worth. SITLA's own papers say:

As zoned today (5 homes max)$3.6M
If rezoned to 100+ lotsabout $16M

The deadline moved. It was Jan. 1, 2025. SITLA's board pushed it to Jan. 1, 2027 on Sept. 19, 2024.

The plan grew. It started as 100 lots on state land. By Sept. 2024 it was part of a roughly 4,000-acre plan. Then the private land became a bid for a new town with 700+ homes, a hotel, glamping and shops.

The county said no. Wasatch County formally rejected the 144-lot agreement on Aug. 19, 2026.

Why it matters

The state is not a bystander here. SITLA earns much more if the county agrees to more homes.

Mike Mower works for Gov. Cox as a senior advisor. He also holds SITLA's at-large board seat. That seat serves "at the pleasure of the Governor" and "does not expire."

To be fair: SITLA's deal terms and dollar amounts were posted in its public board packet. Anyone can read them without a records request.

The board met Aug. 20, 2026. This land was not on the agenda. A different Philo deal, near Bryce Canyon, was.

What happened since

  • Wasatch County passed Resolution 26-11 on Sept. 2, 2026. It asks lawmakers to repeal the law that lets developers start new towns. (Utah News Dispatch, Sept. 21, 2026)
  • The county's report on a pending town application says the draft study "makes no mention of affordable housing." Homes start at $1.5 million. The report does not name the project, but the details match.
  • SITLA's Sept. 17, 2026 board agenda had no item on this deal.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether Philo leaders Chad Staheli, Cory Cozzens or Brett Keenan gave money to Gov. Cox or other candidates. Not checked yet.
  • Whether "Mountain Venture Holdings, LLC" is connected. No link found.
  • Whether Philo will ask SITLA for more time before Jan. 1, 2027.
  • Whether $3.6M and $16M are fair numbers. No one outside SITLA has checked.

Questions to ask

  • SITLA Board of Trustees: Has Philo asked for more time? Did Mike Mower vote on the 2024 extension?
  • Utah Land Trusts Protection and Advocacy Office: Is SITLA getting a fair price for school land?
  • Wasatch County Council: Is any rezone request for this land pending?

Updates since this was posted

  • 2026-08-31 KPCW reported that after an Aug. 28 meeting, Gov. Cox's team asked Wasatch County to consider dropping its planned resolution asking the Legislature to repeal the preliminary-municipality law; the county declined. Councilmember Erik Rowland: "There's not a reality that exists that we're not going to pass that resolution." The county also said it was considering a friend-of-the-court brief in the lawsuit challenging the law. (KPCW (Grace Doerfler), Aug. 31, 2026)
  • 2026-09-02 Wasatch County passed Resolution 26-11 calling on the Legislature to repeal the preliminary-municipality code (S.B. 258, 2024). After the Aug. 28 meeting, the county said there would be "no negative impacts to state funding." (Wasatch County Council agenda, Sept. 2, 2026; Utah News Dispatch, Sept. 21, 2026)
  • 2026-09-02 The county report attached to Resolution 26-11 ("Preliminary Municipalities Report: Flaws in Existing Statutory Processes") says the draft feasibility study it reviewed "makes no mention of affordable housing"; lists homes from $1.5 million (quarter acre) to $10 million (2.5-acre estates), 754 total units, a 200-room hotel, 160 glamping units and 142,115 sq ft of commercial space; says the land carries "little to no existing water rights," relies on onsite septic, and sits entirely in the Wildland Urban Interface; and notes the study assumes 75% full-time occupancy versus about 5% at comparable county developments. The report does not name Wasatch Highlands; it describes "an active preliminary municipality application" whose details match this project. (Wasatch County, Preliminary Municipalities Report (attachment to Sept. 2, 2026 council item))
  • 2026-09-17 SITLA's Sept. 17, 2026 board agenda and packet list no item on DEVL 1297, Mountain Venture Studios or Wasatch County. The approved Aug. 20, 2026 minutes in that packet show the only Philo item was the separate Garfield County transaction near Bryce Canyon (Item 11B). (SITLA Board of Trustees agenda and packet, Sept. 17, 2026 (utah.gov/pmn))
