01 · TERM

SITLA

↻ Tap to flip

The state agency that manages roughly 3.4 million acres of Utah land, granted at statehood specifically to generate money for public schools and other beneficiaries. It leases and sells land to developers; the revenue goes into permanent school funds. Its board is appointed by the Governor.

02 · TERM

Option agreement

↻ Tap to flip

A contract where SITLA agrees it will lease land to a developer, but only once the developer clears certain conditions first — usually getting the local government to rezone the land for denser use. Nothing is final until those conditions are met.

03 · TERM

LLC chain

↻ Tap to flip

One LLC "manages" or "owns" another LLC, which owns another, and so on. Every layer is legal and common in real estate. The reason to care: each layer can be registered in a different state, and some states, Delaware notably, don't require the actual people behind a company to be listed anywhere public.

The chain

Who's actually behind "Mountain Venture Studios"

SITLA's paperwork names its counterparty as "Mountain Venture Studios." That name isn't registered on its own in Utah. Tracing the entities it actually operates through takes four steps.

Where the public trail ends

Philo Venture Partners, LLC's Utah filing left the managers/members field blank — legal, and optional under Utah law. Delaware, its home jurisdiction, doesn't require that information to be public at all. What closes the loop instead: OpenCorporates' officer records independently show Chad Staheli personally listed as manager of that entity and the one above it, filed the same day, Feb. 28, 2024.

The named individuals identified so far
  • Chad Staheli — Co-Founder & CEO, Philo Ventures. Personally named manager on Philo Development Group, LLC and Philo Venture Partners, LLC.
  • Cory Cozzens — Co-Founder & Managing Partner, Philo Ventures.
  • Brett Keenan — CFO & General Counsel, Philo Ventures. Signed the Philo Venture Partners, LLC Utah filing.
  • Greg Whitehead — Partner, Philo Ventures; the public-facing name on the Wasatch Highlands preliminary-municipality application specifically.

The growth pattern

A scope that expanded stage by stage

Not a plan announced all at once — a request that grew, in public records, across four stages.

FEB 2023
SITLA option agreement: rezone to RA-5, minimum 100 lots, just the 720-acre state parcel.
SEPT 2024
SITLA's own memo: the project is now bundled with the Christensen family's 3,500 acres into a roughly 4,000-acre master plan — "increased size and scope," in the memo's own words.
2025–2026
The private land splits off and becomes a preliminary-municipality bid: 700+ homes, a hotel, glamping, retail. The SITLA piece, still nominally 144 units, is pursued separately as a legislative development agreement.
AUG 2026
Wasatch County rejects the 144-lot piece. SITLA's Jan. 2027 deadline still stands — built on a revenue projection assuming 100+ lots, a number already superseded twice by bigger plans.
The state's seat at the table

A Governor's-office staffer sits on SITLA's board

Mike Mower holds SITLA's seventh, at-large board seat — appointed, per SITLA's own board page, "at the pleasure of the Governor," with a term that "does not expire." His day job: Senior Advisor for Community Outreach and Intergovernmental Relations, Governor Cox's office. He remained seated as of the board's Aug. 20, 2026 meeting.

That meeting's agenda — posted a day before Wasatch County's rejection of the 144-lot deal — had no item concerning this parcel at all. A separate, unrelated Philo Development transaction near Bryce Canyon National Park did appear on the same agenda, confirming Philo's relationship with SITLA extends beyond this one deal.
