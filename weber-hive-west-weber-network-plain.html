  • Katrina Gibson's parcel (15-326-0001) transferred to Bennet LLC (Black Pine) on Jan. 8, 2026, the same day she filed her County Commission candidacy. What it is

This case looks at land in West Weber, out by Plain City and Farr West. All of it sits inside a state project area run by the Utah Inland Port Authority, called UIPA.

Two groups of developers work here. One is built around Pat Burns and his company Lync, plus Black Pine (Bennet LLC). The other is Nilson Homes and its JDC Ranch.

Both used the same move. They got more homes approved on the same land. Then part of it was called "affordable" or "attainable." The question here is simple: did adding homes lower prices for buyers, or mostly mean more homes sold near market price?

What the records show

  • The Gibson land. Katrina Gibson's parcel went to Bennet LLC (Black Pine) on Jan. 8, 2026. That is the same day she filed to run for County Commission. The county's own notice says the 550-acre Bennet Farms land is "formerly Gibson Farms."
  • A new zone. The Bennet Farms rezone asks the county to create a brand-new zone, "TN/FB," instead of using one it already has. It also adds an open-space zone along the Weber River.
  • Lots added mid-process. Herrick Estates, a Burns project, grew from 91 lots on 30.65 acres to 93 lots on 32.112 acres between the Planning Commission and the County Commission.
  • JDC Ranch. Nilson Homes first proposed 725 homes on 240 acres. In 2024, the county approved 275 more, called "attainable," for 1,000 total.

Why "attainable" matters.

The "Attainable Starter Home" base price is capped at $400,000. That price does not include a garage, basement, or landscaping. A county planner said on the record they are about 900 square feet.

A regular market-rate townhome in the same community already lists at $399,975, with nothing left out. So the "attainable" home may not be cheaper, and could cost more once it is finished.

To be fair: the attainable homes carry a 10-year deed restriction to keep them away from corporate renters. And the density increase and the label were both openly on the public record.

The program is aimed at first-time buyers, teachers, firefighters and active-duty military. They have the least room to absorb the extra costs.

Three money districts, three different deals

A money district borrows to build roads, sewer and water lines. How it pays the loan back is what matters.

DistrictSizeWho pays it back
PCC PID$150,000,000 debt limitFuture tax growth taken from the school, fire and water districts (50% each) and the county (75%) for 15 years
Sage Creek IFD$8,155,000 in bondsOnly the owners inside the district. Nothing taken from other taxing groups.
Longhorn IFDCosts "exceed $1,000,000"Owner-assessment district; full repayment terms not in the papers reviewed

Sage Creek is the fair version. For it, "this doesn't cost the schools" is true. For PCC PID, it is not.

At PCC PID and Longhorn, owners signed away the public's protest period in writing. Longhorn's board is two-thirds Lync Construction staff, by their own email addresses. One real safeguard: if Longhorn's developer defaults, the zoning goes back to farm use. PCC PID has already grown twice without a new public hearing.

The people with more than one job

  • Stephanie Russell is the county's Economic Development Director, the Manager of the sewer district LMSA, and a paid lobbyist for LMSA through her own business. As director, she got approval to send LMSA's federal sewer money to PCC PID's lift station.
  • At that same meeting, Pat Burns was put on LMSA's board.
  • Jon Beesley was Plain City mayor while he worked for Lync. His forms say "consulted on projects." A text he sent, checked with the person who got it, says "superintendent." His 2025 county form left out four things his Plain City form listed.
  • Kerry Gibson had a paid lobbying contract with Plain City under Beesley for at least 3.5 years.
  • Sharon Bolos, a county commissioner, has a family tie to a Nilson Homes VP that she did not disclose. She voted on Nilson items and praised the JDC Ranch increase.

What we don't know yet

  • What an attainable home really costs once garage, basement and landscaping are added. No closed sale was found.
  • Why the Gibson land moved on the same day she filed.
  • Whether PCC PID's added land overlaps UIPA's tax capture zone. This is not confirmed and is not claimed here.
  • Why Beesley's forms and his text describe his job differently.

The UIPA West Weber area covers 8,967.74 acres. It was adopted May 20, 2024, over a petition with 1,515 signatures. Its plan has one sentence on tribal heritage, next to several pages on wetlands.

Questions to ask

  • Weber County Planning Division and County Commission: what does an attainable home cost once it is finished?
  • Western Weber Planning Commission: did the new TN/FB zone get more review than a normal rezone?
  • Weber County Clerk/Auditor: has Commissioner Bolos ever disclosed her Nilson family tie in a Nilson vote?
  • School, fire and water district boards: how much of your tax growth goes to PCC PID each year?
  • Weber County Commission: will the public get a hearing before a district grows again?
